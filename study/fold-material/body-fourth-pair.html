What changes?

The inherited layer order puts 93 below 55. Each added guard predicts their height gap at a corner of their actual top-view overlap. A positive gap must stay nonnegative; an already negative gap must get no worse. This is stricter than the final geometry tolerance. Both moving triangles and the movement of their overlap corners contribute to the prediction.

The twelve old overlap guards and the vertex 27–plane 70 guard keep their original order. Six new overlap guards follow them. No vertex 30 plane guard is added. Material preferences, held vertices, cost weights and solver budgets stay unchanged.

A direction is a proposed numerical correction of all free vertices. We try fractions 1, 1/2, 1/4, and so on, up to 31 choices. A refused fraction may receive the same single vertex 27–plane 70 repair as before. The repair must also preserve the new overlap guards, including fresh predictions at the refused shape. The first passing raw or repaired shape is selected. These fractions are not physical folding steps.

Scope of this comparison

One connected sheet retains 87 shared vertices and 120 triangles. The preceding archive is checked before this experiment, including all 137 loop states and 30 saved repairs. The old direction is replayed and its decisions must match. Source bytes are preserved in the gallery's source directory. There is one new material direction, with no continuation from its selected state.

This tests a local contact choice. It does not establish a settled body opening, a continuously checked flexible movement, or the response of the rest of the crane. Larger fractions with bad geometry remain visible for diagnosis.

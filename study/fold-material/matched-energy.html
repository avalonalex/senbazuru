Edge locationsCountEnergy change

A shared location has the same material edge endpoints, regardless of vertex numbering. Its neighboring triangles may differ after refinement. An added edge has no old spring; its absence does not mean that the older paper was flat there.

Separate the coefficient and angle terms at shared locations

For a passive spring, E = k θ² / 2: k is its coefficient and θ its measured turn. The coefficient depends on material edge length and the heights of adjacent triangles. The exact symmetric split is ΔE = (k₁ − k₀)(θ₀² + θ₁²)/4 + (k₀ + k₁)(θ₁² − θ₀²)/4.

This is algebraic accounting, not independent physical causes. The angles span different material neighborhoods, and the stored shapes also differ. Added and removed springs remain separate.

What this isolates

The interior refinement adds bending locations close to the held strip. Their positive costs outweigh reductions at existing edges; the held boundary also becomes cheaper. Adding only the outer strip leaves most of this difference intact.

This narrows the next question to the discrete bending rule and its boundary response. It does not prove a continuum limit, calibrate real paper, change acceptance thresholds, or choose an illustration tolerance. A prescribed-shape check on these same nonuniform meshes can separate how the energy rule changes from how the solved shape changes.

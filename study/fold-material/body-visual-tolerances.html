A pixel budget is a review aid, not a collision certificate. A tiny negative gap may cover a broad region, and that region may be buried. Inspect exposed colours, creases and silhouette before accepting an illustrative pose. Magnification helps locate a defect; it does not make it important at drawing size.

Annotations: orange is the lower-surface footprint where the required upper layer is deeper than the selected allowance. Red marks intersections from the strictly failing triangle pairs whose 3D intersection length exceeds that allowance. Red strokes are deliberately widened to locate them. Neither overlay hides buried paper. The screen compares two different distances, not a unified contact tolerance.

Saved cases on the same ruler

No new solve runs, and none of these saved poses is claimed to be a converged material solution. One pixel is 1/600 of the sheet side. For an illustrative 150 mm sheet, that is 0.25 mm; the existing 1e-7 contact distance is 0.00006 px (15 nm). These are scale conversions, not paper-thickness assumptions. Absolute edge error is the error in an individual mesh edge, not a bound on the overall silhouette. Angle-derived grips differ from the other cases, so their shapes are controls, not a matched solver comparison.

What the distance screen would overlook

“Below” means only the three scalar maxima above fit that screen. It does not accept the pose, change historical verdicts, or verify the visible layering. Strain remains separate; a mesh can accumulate many small edge errors. The area below sums pair footprints and can count overlaps more than once; it is not exposed orange ink area.

Proposed next gate: use 0.1 px as a conservative distance review screen at this scale, with a separate 0.1% local strain screen. A case below those screens still needs a credible rendered layer order. Keep 0.5 and 1 px as sensitivity comparisons. Spend further solver precision only where the intended drawing or useful body opening benefits; a renderer fallback is a rendering limitation to resolve, not evidence to run more nanometre contact repairs.

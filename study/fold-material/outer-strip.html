Four meshes, one numerical policy

The width stays fixed. Coarse uses the original 2×1 grid; fully refined uses 4×1. Outer-only adds the material column at 15/32 to coarse; rest-only removes that column from fine. The strip beside the shared crease and the outer edge remain held.

All solves use the same six length weights through 1e10, progressive contact exchange and 40 iterations per stage. A passed endpoint requires convergence, relative edge error ≤ 1e-5, original crease error ≤ 1e-5 radians, exact holds/material identities, independent whole-sheet contact at 1e-7 and nonnegative exact directional gaps. These are static checks, not a checked folding route.

Each material strip diagram shows one unfolded panel: green is held near the crease; pale ochre is the short outer strip. Band support on the uneven meshes

The band remains at 1/8…7/16. Each transverse spring represents the interval halfway to its neighboring material columns. Clipping that interval to the band fixes its preferred turn and stiffness. The original rule measures the full angle; the fractional rule measures the covered fraction of actual and preferred turn.

At the band end, the covered fraction is 1/2 on both uniform grids, 2/3 on outer-only and 1/3 on rest-only. The equivalent spring coefficients therefore change under the fractional rule. Physical loading is fixed; individual spring matrices are not. Passive stiffness also follows the changed triangle dimensions.

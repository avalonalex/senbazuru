Creases remember; panels resist

A crease prefers a particular angle. The paper between creases prefers to stay flat. These examples let the connected sheet settle under both preferences. Opposing flaps add contact correction: their preferred angles would make them cross. A curled strip now tests contact between two regions of one uncreased panel. Separate bending controls curl it; local contact requirements keep its returning end above its starting end.

What this checks

The same indexed vertices join neighboring triangles throughout the solve. Original sheet coordinates and triangle connections stay fixed. The final maximum edge-length error must be at most 0.001%, and reversed layer gaps at most 0.0000001 of the unit sheet’s side. “Settled” additionally requires the solver’s full proposed movement to be below that same distance. Merely reaching the iteration limit does not pass.

The original single/double-fold solver enforces the known packet order. Diagonal and kite examples solve lengths and angular energy without contact forces; the opposing-flap example adds forces for declared panel order along a fixed direction in the model. A separate check inspects every pair of triangles in each saved state for strict crossings, unresolved coplanar overlap and declared layer order. The opposing flaps use a numerical clearance of 0.000001 sheet units between panels that share no material vertices. The discovered-contact example learns order from overlapping projections in its separated reference pose, and rescans each candidate shape. A new overlap must have an order already implied by that reference; otherwise it is refused. Ambiguous or crossed references need more information. The first-contact example can extend this history at supplied, angle-defined approach poses. New relationships are accepted only after the pose passes earlier orders and an independent triangle check. Rejected observations leave the history unchanged. Each of its four approach intervals now has a specified rigid rotation about the right crease. Bounds over the whole angular interval check separation; a collision or unresolved interval blocks the observation. This does not check the numerical correction trajectory or arbitrary bending. The curled-panel example instead names nonadjacent material triangles inside one panel. Their local lower/upper order is supplied explicitly and stays fixed during correction. Triangles sharing a material vertex cannot receive these separating requirements. This does not discover arbitrary self-contact or guarantee collision-free solver motion. Joined panels keep zero clearance. This margin is not physical paper thickness. Other motions, finite-thickness contact and global optimality remain outside these checks. Numerical iterations are not folding instructions. Only the original packets receive a display-depth tie-break after their contact check passes; the saved positions do not change.

Stiffness and energy use illustrative units. The crease stiffness is 1; the double-fold examples compare panel stiffnesses 0.2 and 5 on the same mesh. This is a first angular-spring experiment, not a calibrated prediction for a particular paper stock.

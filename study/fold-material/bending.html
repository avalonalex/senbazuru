Creases remember; panels resist

A crease prefers a particular angle. The paper between creases prefers to stay flat. These examples let the connected sheet settle under both preferences. Opposing flaps add contact correction: their preferred angles would make them cross. The newest example starts with flaps whose projections do not overlap, records their first separated overlap, then keeps the learned order during correction.

What this checks

The same indexed vertices join neighboring triangles throughout the solve. Original sheet coordinates and triangle connections stay fixed. The final maximum edge-length error must be at most 0.001%, and reversed layer gaps at most 0.0000001 of the unit sheet’s side. “Settled” additionally requires the solver’s full proposed movement to be below that same distance. Merely reaching the iteration limit does not pass.

The original single/double-fold solver enforces the known packet order. Diagonal and kite examples solve lengths and angular energy without contact forces; the opposing-flap example adds forces for declared panel order along a fixed direction in the model. A separate check inspects every pair of triangles in each saved state for strict crossings, unresolved coplanar overlap and declared layer order. The opposing flaps use a numerical clearance of 0.000001 sheet units between panels that share no material vertices. The discovered-contact example learns order from overlapping projections in its separated reference pose, and rescans each candidate shape. A new overlap must have an order already implied by that reference; otherwise it is refused. Ambiguous or crossed references need more information. The first-contact example can extend this history at supplied, angle-defined approach poses. New relationships are accepted only after the pose passes earlier orders and an independent triangle check. Rejected observations leave the history unchanged. A crossing and separation between samples can still be missed. Joined panels keep zero clearance. This margin is not physical paper thickness. These diagnostics do not certify collision-free motion, finite thickness, or a globally optimal shape. Numerical iterations are not folding instructions. Only the original packets receive a display-depth tie-break after their contact check passes; the saved positions do not change.

Stiffness and energy use illustrative units. The crease stiffness is 1; the double-fold examples compare panel stiffnesses 0.2 and 5 on the same mesh. This is a first angular-spring experiment, not a calibrated prediction for a particular paper stock.

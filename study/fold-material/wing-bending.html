A wing that bends

Hold the wide root of a triangular sheet. Turn and move a small grip near its tip. The paper between them settles into a curve while resisting bending and stretching. This is one uncreased sheet, shaped like a wing.

Could paper take these shapes?

These are the three shapes at the mesh chosen above. The wing is one sheet unit long, root to tip, and the figures are drawn at 320 px to the unit, so a floor in their own pixels is 0.53 times the figure in the table. The 16-division mesh is the 8-division mesh with every triangle split into four, and the gallery solves each grip on both, so at 8 divisions a shape's finer level is its grip solved at 16, where that solve counts as the shape made again by the rule above (owner decisions 29 and 31). The table says how far apart the two are, or that the solve does not count. The 16- and 24-division shapes have no such solve, and their finer level is not measured (owner decision 27).

A strip with a known answer

Equal-length strip spans with equal turns give an exactly length-preserving bent shape. With the two end regions held, the solver starts from a perturbed version and recovers that reference. This checks the positional controls and the bending model before applying them to the wider wing.

A strip is drawn only in 3D, with no camera of its own, so its screen has no picture floor. Each strip holds its first and last span, which shorten as spans are added, so no two strips solve the same control, and no strip has a finer level (owner decision 27).

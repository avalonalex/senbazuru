Two petals become a bird base

Start with the square base: a collapsed square with several touching layers. Lift its top tip and guide both sides inward. The result is one long, narrow flap called a petal. Hold it nearly flat, fold the back petal underneath, then press both flat to complete the bird base.

Seven creases move together within each petal. Two old folds open as four side folds close and the tip rises. All three stages are checked throughout their motion, including leaving the square base and the final flat landing.

What was checked

All 120 panel pairs and all 28 material edge lengths in each of three stages, together with the approach and departure order of touching layers. Both stage joins preserve positions, crease angles, material identities and layer requirements. Exact arithmetic checks the ideal motion between the pictures. Every displayed pose is rebuilt from crease angles and agrees with that path within 0.000000000001 of the sheet’s side length; shared vertices, achieved angles and contact are checked again.

This is zero-thickness paper following one known two-petal route. It does not yet check the initial square-base collapse or find a route for an arbitrary crease pattern. Complete paper in the 3D viewer retains coincident buried layers; Visible paper removes them for stable display.

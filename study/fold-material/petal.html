SENBAZURU · CHECKED FOLDING SEQUENCE

An open sheet becomes a bird base

Start with a square sheet carrying the prepared crease pattern. Bring its sides together into a smaller, layered square: the square base. Lift the front tip while guiding both sides inward to make a long, narrow flap called a petal. Repeat underneath, then press both petals flat.

The collapse, both petals and the final press are checked throughout their motion. The same connected sheet runs through all four stages, with no change of material identity at the joins.

What was checked

All 120 panel pairs and all 28 material edge lengths in each of four stages, together with the approach and departure order of touching layers. All three stage joins preserve positions, crease angles, material identities and layer requirements. During collapse, tilted panels can pass above one another in projection while remaining separated in space; their layer order is checked as they approach the flat landing. Exact arithmetic checks the ideal motion between the pictures. Every displayed pose is rebuilt from crease angles and agrees with that path within 0.000000000001 of the sheet’s side length; shared vertices, achieved angles and contact are checked again.

This is zero-thickness paper following one known route from a prepared flat sheet to the bird base. Making those pre-creases, paper bending and finding a route for an arbitrary pattern remain outside this example. Complete paper in the 3D viewer retains coincident buried layers; Visible paper removes them for stable display.

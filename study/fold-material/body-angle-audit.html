What this tells us

The selected response matrix has nearly dependent combinations: some equations respond almost the same way to a change. Rounding can therefore upset their cancellations. Force balance alone looked excellent because the old direction was constructed from those forces; it did not establish that the selected equalities were satisfied accurately.

The original method stops when the same selected equations return an identical floating-point direction. That explains the early refusal after 34 of 200 iterations; raising the iteration budget alone would not change that calculation.

The next bounded comparison is a more stable way to evaluate this same selected system, checked against the exact audit. No tolerance waiver, damping change or new pair-specific constraint is needed to explain this failure. A successful linear direction would still need actual geometry trials and all material/contact checks. This audit supplies no new opened body or barrier-ready start.

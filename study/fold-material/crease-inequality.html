A constrained pass requires solver convergence, relative edge-length error ≤ 1e-5, unchanged holds, crease error ≤ 1e-5 radians, the existing whole-sheet contact check, and an exact minimum lower/upper gap ≥ 0. The whole-sheet check keeps its 1e-7 distance tolerance. Exact fractions describe the stored coordinates, not uncertain physical paper measurements.

How does the constraint work?

Clipping an upper triangle over the fixed lower panel gives small overlap polygons. The height difference is linear within each piece, so checking every corner bounds the entire piece. Each corner retains its weights on the upper vertices. The material solver uses those weights to constrain its next move. A separated contact supplies no pulling force.

A proposed move can change the overlaps. We clip again and, if needed, raise all free upper vertices by the smallest common height that removes negative gaps. Heights round toward the permitted side. The line search measures material energy after this repair; it accepts only a decrease. The initial repair is reported separately, and the JSON records every accepted repair.

This is a controlled experiment with a fixed lower panel and a known layer order. Moving both panels, discovering contact between arbitrary folds, and showing a physically possible route still need further work.

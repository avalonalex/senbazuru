Two layers, one bend

Fold a diamond in half along its middle crease. Hold the wide root and the narrow tip of both layers, then turn the tip grips together. The paper between them can bend, while the upper layer must stay above the lower one.

Touching is allowed

These are two halves of one connected sheet. Only points along the original root crease are shared. Everywhere else, two points that occupy the same place still belong to different parts of the paper and can move apart.

1 · Flat grips2 · Both grips at 20°3 · Both grips at 40°

The paper has zero thickness here. When both layers touch exactly, their outside outline looks like a single surface. The declared layer order chooses which side is visible. No display gap is added.

Contact does not glue the layers

Lift only the upper grip by 0.01 model units—one percent of the root-to-tip length. The upper layer can separate, while the original root crease stays closed. The solver holds the grips exactly and corrects the free paper.

The other control starts with the free upper layer slightly inside the lower layer. Its corrected endpoint must pass a separate contact check. A grip deliberately held underneath the lower one is incompatible with the declared order and is reported as a failure.

Check the shape and the solve

“Accepted” requires solver convergence, fixed grips, a closed root crease and an independent contact check over every triangle pair. Area and principal strain (stretch along the most and least stretched directions) are also recorded in the measurements download. A small length error on its own does not establish that the paper has settled.

Inspect the accepted surfaces

SVG, FOLD and glTF use the same solved positions. The default glTF scene removes buried coplanar paper; “Complete paper” retains both layers for inspection. Internal triangle joins are subdivisions, not new creases. The root remains the one original crease.

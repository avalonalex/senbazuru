The body is connected around its sides too

The eight panels meeting the square's centre form the body core in this map. The surrounding sectors also join each other along their base strips. They are not four independent flaps attached only to that core.

Each listed edge has two material owners. A supplied layer order between overlapping panels is a different relationship: it does not glue them together. The unchanged crane retains all source layer orders, including the tucked tail.

Four points meet underneath

The magenta crosses mark the four midpoints of the original paper's outer edges. In the folded-flat crane they coincide underneath, but remain four different material vertices. Their separation is a candidate measurement for a later opening experiment.

The core's 16 perimeter edges are internal connections to more paper. They are not an exposed rim. This map does not yet identify a closed cavity, a complete mouth boundary or an inflation volume. Adding a cap across those edges would invent a surface.

Which angles should be allowed to change?

The orange lines are original mountain/valley creases either within the body core or crossing a region interface. They form a candidate set for opening; this is not a proof that every one must move, or that the set is sufficient. A mountain/valley assignment identifies the fold direction, while its achieved angle can change.

What this checks

Region selection uses original-sheet coordinates and shared-edge incidence, never similarity of folded positions. All five regions are connected along material edges. The FOLD and 3D downloads preserve the starting surface.

Scope: a checked material map of the existing crane. It does not open the body, solve equilibrium, prove a folding route, or predict pressure. The body core is a candidate region to study, not an automatically recovered physical cavity.

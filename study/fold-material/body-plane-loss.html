Two movements cancel—until they act together

The guard predicts distance using the initial plane orientation and its initial rate of turning. Relative vertex movement and plane rotation cancel in that prediction. During the finite trial, the plane is already turning while the vertex moves relative to it. Their interaction can still reduce the distance.

The first-order cancellation

We measure the vertex relative to triangle 70's centroid—the average of its three corners. A normal is a unit-length arrow perpendicular to the plane. The distance is the component of the relative position along that normal. The two linear terms below nearly cancel for the additional guard. What the linear prediction misses

Interaction measures the changed normal acting on the changed relative position. Plane rotation remainder measures the normal's departure from its initial turning rate, acting on the original relative position. The reference point is fixed by our centroid convention; individual shares are not unique physical causes.

The exact accounting identity

Let r0 be the initial vertex-minus-centroid vector and n0 the initial normal. For the saved trial, dr = r − r0 and dn = n − n0. Then d − d0 = n0·dr + dn·r0 + dn·dr. Subtracting the initial linear prediction leaves the rotation remainder, interaction and relative rounding shown above. The second-order normal includes its normalization, so its length stays consistent with a unit normal to this order.

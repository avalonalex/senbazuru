Choose the reference

Every profile uses the same camera and scale. Grey is the continuous reference; teal and dashed rust are the two coincident material panels. Only the real central crease shares vertices. These static references are neither a folding route nor equilibria of the old grip problem.

Exact samples put matching material points on the same curve, but the straight edges between them are shorter than the material arcs. Full-length edges keep each strip's material length and chord direction; on uneven meshes each strip needs its own construction. Matching points then move slightly. The old outer grips are not imposed in either version.

Account for the passive energy

Passive energy is the model's cost of bending a panel. For these references its continuous comparison value is 0.1 × curvature² × bent length, with curvature 1.2 radians per sheet length and panel width one. This is a reference for the current rule, not a measured paper stiffness or energy in joules.

What this tells us

The existing rule changes energy even when we prescribe the reference. The measured changes match explicit boundary and transition terms; switching from chords to full-length edges preserves angular costs while removing length error. This identifies a discretization effect without explaining the entire 8.99% change in the optimized matched shapes.

The references have no width-varying bend or layer separation and do not keep the old outer grips. Their exact touching-order checks use the known two-panel arrangement. They do not calibrate real paper, certify general self-contact or flexible motion, select a new default material rule, or relax an illustration criterion.

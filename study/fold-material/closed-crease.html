One crease, two bending panels

Fold a square in half, almost closed. Let each half bend while keeping every piece of paper the same size. With only one shared crease, we can tell exactly where the two layers touch, separate or cross.

A small shape with a known answer

Each panel follows four straight segments, extended along the crease. The segments can turn without stretching. Their joins approximate flexible panel bending; only the fold joining the two halves is an authored crease. The shared line uses three common vertices. Touching material elsewhere keeps separate identities.

This diagnostic FOLD. Coordinates retain the measured shape; failed controls are included for inspection.

These shapes are prescribed mathematical controls. They are not solved equilibria, and switching between them is not a checked folding motion. Refining the mesh subdivides the same four segments; it does not smooth or change the ideal shape.

Does the numerical check agree?

The exact profile is a separate reference: positive upper-minus-lower height means ordered separation; zero means touching; negative means reversed order. A change of sign inside the panels means a crossing. The production checker inspects all triangle pairs and the declared lower/upper order.

A nearby point can invent a crossing

The investigation also found a concrete checker bug. Two triangles lie in nearly parallel planes. The line where those planes meet is x = 0. One triangle reaches y = 10/21 on that line; the other starts at y = 39/80. Their intervals are separated by 19/1680, so the triangles do not cross.

A corner off the line reaches y = 1/2. It is only 1.25e-8 from the other plane, so the old checker treated it as on the cut and invented an overlap. The corrected checker constructs the actual plane cuts, then applies the existing crossing tolerances. Moving the second triangle's start to y = 15/32 produces a real crossing and remains rejected.

This fixes a false report without repairing an intersecting mesh. Rechecking the saved crane shapes reduces the held-crease case from 19 crossing reports to 13; the original still has 49. All previously failed shapes still fail contact. The larger body-opening problem remains open.

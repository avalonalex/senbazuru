The blue vertex and the green corner

Vertex 27 belongs to triangle 46. Its top-view position is just outside triangle 70. The crossing test measures its signed perpendicular distance to triangle 70's extended plane: imagine that triangle's flat surface continuing beyond its edges. The sign follows the triangle's written vertex order; it does not directly mean above or below the paper.

The gap corner lies inside the actual top-view overlap. The contact guard measures the upper layer's height minus the lower layer's height there. Triangle 70 is the declared upper layer. A negative gap is a small local reversal of that order. Extending the plane to vertex 27 does not make that vertex another physical overlap corner.

What changes at the threshold?

The plane test needs vertices farther than 1e-7 sheet units on both sides of each triangle's plane, plus a long enough intersection. Triangle 70 already spans both sides of plane 46; the intersection already exceeds 1e-7. Vertex 27 supplies the last missing condition for triangle 46 against plane 70.

This is a measurement plot, not a deformation of the paper. It shows vertex distance + 1e-7, in units of 1e-12 sheet lengths. Positive means that vertex remains within the plane tolerance; negative means it has crossed the threshold. The tiny refused margin is about −0.07754 in these units. A separate 60-digit calculation confirms its sign for the saved coordinates.

What this tells us

A smaller mathematical intersection already exists in the retained shape. The refusal is a numerical threshold transition, not a sudden new visible collision. The gap guard alone cannot prevent it: its four overlap corners do not include vertex 27. This does not show that the whole-triangle check is wrong, or justify weakening its tolerance.

The next bounded experiment can compare one direction that also controls vertex 27's plane distance, at this same saved starting shape. That would be a conservative local guard: the vertex is outside the finite triangle. Every displaced trial would still need the existing all-pair checks.

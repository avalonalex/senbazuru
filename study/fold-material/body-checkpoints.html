Where did the body patch struggle?

The same two 5° openings, remeasured at the positions saved during their original runs. No new solves, changed holds or relaxed geometry checks. The patch contains the body core and both wing roots; the rest of the crane is absent.

Iteration zero is an imperfect starting guess. The solver first uses a weak length penalty, then increases it every 40 corrections. Early strain can be temporary. Only the final endpoint is considered for acceptance, and neither opened run was recorded as converged.

The finding: the starting guesses already reverse some layers. Both runs develop crossings in their first correction. The coarse run recovers geometry by its last saved point, after a misleading near-stall. The refined run keeps descending in cost, but none of its saved snapshots passes geometry. Refinement also changes the sampling of the contact penalty; that is a possible contributor, not a proven cause.

What the costs tell us

Crease cost measures departure from preferred fold angles; panel cost measures bending between triangles inside a panel. Length and contact costs are numerical penalties that grow as the solve tightens its constraints. They are separate from paper stiffness. The displayed total is half the original line-search objective.

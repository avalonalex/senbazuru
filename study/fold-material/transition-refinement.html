One fixed placement rule

Both policies start with the same 64-triangle mesh. Local refinement repeatedly bisects the interval with the greatest weighted length: density four within 1/32 sheet length of either curvature transition, one outside. Equal scores choose the interval nearer the shared crease. The two width strips never change. Each curve supplies its own fixed transition locations; no measured energy is used to choose columns or tune the windows.

What the comparison means

This is one predeclared placement policy on two fixed, width-independent profiles. It can improve one cost while worsening another measurement; it is not an optimal grid or an adaptive solver. All controls remain diagnostic, including any sampled mesh inside the numerical length cap. Material stiffness, triangle weighting and acceptance criteria are unchanged. The solved 8.99% gap, realistic force response, separated layers and a continuously checked flexible motion remain separate work.

When the panels want different shapes

Give the upper half of a folded sheet a different grip or bend preference. The two layers may separate, or one may push its partner. Contact allows touching and prevents crossing; it does not glue the layers together.

Keep the crease closed; let the interiors respond

A narrow strip beside the shared crease stays held on both sides, as does each outer edge. The three shared crease vertices remain one connection; the touching layers elsewhere retain distinct material identities. The compatible controls below use these same holding regions.

Holding the strip matters: moving an upper grip with only the crease line held also opens the crease. This experiment holds that orientation explicitly so panel bending can be judged against the original closed crease.

What are the unequal controls?

The opened upper grip is a 0.05-radian turn about the end of the held root strip. Its starting shape preserves lengths, but it remains a static guess. The bend preference instead acts at three upper material lines, asking for twice their original profile turn. These extra angular springs have the same strength per line length at both resolutions. They neither add authored creases nor change the lower panel’s material.

The contact-off comparison removes only contact inequalities and their feasibility repairs. It keeps the same upper springs and all holds. Because the shared crease and its adjacent triangles stay fixed, this comparison separates the influence of contact from a moving shared crease. Reaching equilibrium without contact can produce a physically invalid crossing.

The repair, when enabled, moves free upper and lower vertices apart by a recorded common amount per side. Exact gaps are rechecked after rounding, and energy must decrease after repair. Known z order and the original triangle facing directions are still required. This does not establish calibrated paper stiffness, mesh independence, arbitrary contact or a continuous flexible route.

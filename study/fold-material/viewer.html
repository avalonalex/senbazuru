Senbazuru · material study / #114

One sheet, two bends

A square folded once, then in half again at right angles. Compare sharp creases with exaggerated rounded bends. The layers meet at their folds; they need not stay parallel away from them.

These are prescribed geometric shapes, not a physics simulation. Both double-fold constructions distort the original sheet. No material stiffness, solid thickness, contact or equilibrium is solved.

What this experiment measures

The sharp single fold rotates one rigid half around its crease. The sharp double fold uses gaps that close at the creases and grow towards the free edges. Two of the panels bend into gently curved surfaces. That captures a drawing target, but their measured extension means the shape is not an unstretched square.

The rounded single fold consumes a strip of width πr, with r = 0.015. The second rounded bend uses the same strip for radii r and 3r, making the outer strip three times as long: 200% extension. Its layer separation is exaggerated to 3% of the original sheet's side. The sharp example instead specifies a 2.5% free-edge gap; that gap is not paper thickness.

Edge extension compares each mesh edge's 3D length with its original length. Chords are slightly shorter than circular arcs, producing the rounded single fold's small compression. The heat map shows analytic local extension at each triangle's material centroid: maximum over directions for sharp panels, and along the second fold for rounded bends. The color ranges differ between shapes and are printed below the view.

All measurements and model positions come from Haskell. Shared vertex indices connect the surface; the viewer splits lighting normals at sharp creases without splitting the material. Connectivity and length checks alone do not establish contact or physical feasibility.

Edge locationsCountEnergy change

A shared location has the same material edge endpoints, regardless of vertex numbering. Its neighboring triangles may differ after refinement. An added edge has no old spring; its absence does not mean that the older paper was flat there.

Separate the coefficient and angle terms at shared locations

For a passive spring, E = k θ² / 2: k is its coefficient and θ its measured turn. The coefficient depends on material edge length and the heights of adjacent triangles. The exact symmetric split is ΔE = (k₁ − k₀)(θ₀² + θ₁²)/4 + (k₀ + k₁)(θ₁² − θ₀²)/4.

This is algebraic accounting, not independent physical causes. The angles span different material neighborhoods, and the stored shapes also differ. Added and removed springs remain separate.

What this isolates

In both 128 → 256 refinements, the held-boundary cost falls. Increases in the first free interval and farther through the free panel outweigh that reduction. The discrepancy is distributed. At equal triangle counts, whole-bend placement lowers costs in all three nonzero bins.

Triangle spacing changes both the coefficient and the angle a spring measures. Lower cost does not establish a more accurate equilibrium. The 5% cost and 0.001-sheet-length shape targets remain unchanged; width refinement, tighter-solve confirmation and real-paper calibration remain untested. The next bounded check is tighter-solve confirmation of the two 256-triangle endpoints, preserving these originals and the current material, grips and acceptance caps.

Can small valid corrections accumulate?

The previous experiment found one usable eighth-step at the saved body shape. Here each retained shape gets a fresh local prediction of how to reduce bending, stretching and contact costs. We keep the same three contact pairs and stop after at most ten corrections. This is a numerical search for a plausible shape, not a sequence of physical folding instructions.

The retained chain

One correction means proposing a full displacement, checking that quadratic calculation, and trying the same 31 fractions from 1 down to 1/2³⁰. The first fraction that lowers cost and passes every geometry check may be retained. The next attempt starts there and recomputes all material rows and the three pairs' overlap corners. An unverified quadratic or exhausted search stops the chain without replacing its last valid shape.

Refreshed contact constraints

A guard describes the signed height gap at one corner of the projected overlap: positive means the declared upper layer lies above the lower. For 22–63, 63 is above 22; for 14–55, 14 is above 55; for 46–70, 70 is above 46. A tiny tolerated negative gap cannot worsen in the local prediction. Actual trial geometry still receives independent checks, because finite changes can differ from that prediction.

Unchanged material and acceptance limits

One connected sheet, exact holds, relative edge-length error ≤ 1e-5, and contact/order checks at 1e-7 sheet units. All pairs are checked, even though only three pairs supply guards. Full proposed movement must be ≤ 1e-7 for equilibrium; a small retained fraction alone does not qualify. No material stiffness, force weight, stopping threshold or order requirement changes during this run.

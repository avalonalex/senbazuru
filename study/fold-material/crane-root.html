A hold and a preference are different

A hold fixes material points exactly. A crease spring prefers an angle, but can settle at another angle as the rest of the sheet resists. All controls keep the same 50° tip grip. Releasing the root strip leaves only the body and the tip held.

These are physical mesh comparisons: the renderer, material identities and layer orders are unchanged. No display gap or smoothing of crease normals is used to disguise the transition.

Give nearby body paper room to move

The last body control releases the four material panels directly across the wing-root edges. Material shared with any other panel stays held, so the distant boundary, other wing, neck and tucked tail remain in place. The source layer orders also constrain these newly moving body panels.

A failed numerical solve does not prove that a compatible smoother crane cannot exist. It identifies a limit of this particular grip, held region, material model and solve budget. Its FOLD download is a diagnostic, not an accepted paper shape.

What passes the checks?

Acceptance requires solver equilibrium, relative material edge errors at most 0.001%, exact remaining holds, and an independent check of all triangle contacts and 902 source layer orders. Original mountain/valley crease angles must stay within 0.00001 radians. The four wing-root springs are measured separately: their preferred angles are deliberately soft.

Does refinement change the conclusion?

Both sides of the root receive dense triangles: four wing panels and four neighbouring body panels. Every inserted midpoint is shared by its material neighbours. The flat-preference control repeats with sixteen spans per source edge instead of eight, with the same held regions and stiffness.

Scope: independently checked static endpoints. Numerical iterates can stretch or cross paper and are not a flexible folding route. The illustrative stiffness is not fitted to a real paper sample. Two-wing spreading, body inflation and friction remain later studies.

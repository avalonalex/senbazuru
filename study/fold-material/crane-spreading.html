Can the body stay fixed?

For this modest held bend, yes. Acceptance requires unchanged body and grip positions, relative edge-length error at most 0.001%, original crease angles within 0.00001 radians, solver convergence, and an independent contact check over every triangle pair. All retained source layer orders, including the tail between the body layers, are checked.

The invalid control pushes an upper tip grip through its lower partner. Its exact grip cannot be moved to conceal the conflict; the failed shape is saved for diagnosis and excluded from the model gallery. It uses two iterations per penalty stage; the regular controls allow twenty.

Contact uses a distance tolerance of 0.0000001 model units. A seam overlap narrower than that margin counts as edge contact. Wider unordered overlap, reversed layers and crossings remain failures. This tolerance is numerical; the sheet still has zero physical thickness.

Does a finer wing agree?

Only the four material panels forming this wing receive dense refinement. Neighbours split where they share a newly divided edge, keeping the sheet connected. The held regions and stiffness stay the same in both solves.

Bending energy measures this model's resistance to curvature. The stiffness is illustrative, not calibrated to a particular paper. Two resolutions provide a useful comparison, but do not establish a resolution-independent answer or predict the shape of an unheld crane.

Scope: one gently curved, held wing on the connected crane. The numerical solve can stretch or cross paper on its way to an endpoint; these results do not certify a flexible folding route. Freer wing spreading, body reshaping, pressure, friction and physical paper thickness need further material study.
